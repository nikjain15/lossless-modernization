# Learning Paths: Where to Start

**Exec summary.** This repo is a reference, not a novel: nobody should read it front to back. Pick your role and your time budget below, and follow the ordered list in that cell. Leaders start with why programs fail, because the failure data is what justifies doing this differently. Architects start with the three decisions, because a wrong strategy choice invalidates everything downstream. Engineers start with the patterns and templates, because their job is execution. Everyone ends at the [readiness scorecard](assessment/readiness-scorecard.md): it turns reading into a diagnosis of your own program.

## Notes per role

### CTO / engineering leader

Your decision is whether to fund this, how to gate it, and what "done" means. Start with the failure library because it is your negotiation leverage: 79% of modernization projects fail [vFunction/Wakefield, 2022](https://info.vfunction.com/hubfs/Download%20Assets/Wakefield-Report-2022-Why-App-Modernization-Projects-Fail.pdf), and the expensive ones fail at cutover, not at kickoff. Every doc opens with an exec summary written for you; read those first and go deeper only where your program scores red on the [scorecard](assessment/readiness-scorecard.md). The single most useful thing you can take from a week: define go-live as parity threshold plus time-in-parallel plus business sign-off, and put your name on that gate.

### Architect

You own the three decisions in [decide/](decide/README.md) and the artifact lifecycle in [playbook/README.md](playbook/README.md). Read the decision trees before the patterns: a correct strangler-fig execution of the wrong strategy still fails. By end of week you should have a filled [application inventory](templates/application-inventory.md) with 7R dispositions, an [ADR](templates/adr.md) for your replacement approach, and a point of view on the [parity harness](patterns/parity-harness-deepdive.md) as the program's spine rather than its QA phase.

### Hands-on engineer

Your work is comprehension and evidence. The pattern docs are the how; the templates are the deliverables your program will actually be judged on. Fill templates against a real system as you read, starting with the [characterization test plan](templates/characterization-test-plan.md): pinning current behavior before changing code is the first move on any legacy estate [Feathers, 2004](https://www.goodreads.com/en/book/show/44919.Working_Effectively_with_Legacy_Code). The [flagship case study](case-studies/flagship-program.md) shows every pattern and template used together on one real program.
